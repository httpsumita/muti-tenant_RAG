## 1. Purpose

This Company Policy Handbook establishes the standards, responsibilities, and procedures that govern employees, managers, administrators, and other authorized personnel within the organization. Its purpose is to create a consistent workplace environment while protecting employees, company resources, confidential information, and organizational systems.

The handbook defines expectations regarding workplace conduct, working hours, leave, information security, access control, document management, remote work, performance, communication, and compliance. Every employee is responsible for understanding and following the policies applicable to their role.

The organization may update these policies when business requirements, regulations, security practices, or operational procedures change. Employees are expected to review policy updates and comply with the latest approved version.

This document is owned and maintained by the HR and Administration team. Access to the handbook is controlled through role-based permissions. Certain sections or related documents may contain more sensitive information and may therefore have more restrictive access requirements.

## 2. Tenant and Document Ownership

All company documents must belong to a specific organizational tenant. A tenant represents the organization or business unit that owns the information.

For this handbook, the tenant is:

**Tenant:** `tenant-a`

Every document stored within the company's document management platform must have a unique document ID and a descriptive title. These identifiers allow documents to be tracked, updated, audited, and retrieved without ambiguity.

Each document must also have an owner. The owner is responsible for maintaining the accuracy of the document, coordinating revisions, and ensuring that access permissions remain appropriate.

For this handbook:

**Document ID:** `DOC-TENA-001`  
**Owner:** `HR and Administration`

Employees must not change ownership, tenant information, or access permissions unless they have been explicitly authorized to do so.

Documents must never be moved between tenants without an approved administrative process. Tenant boundaries are mandatory because they prevent information belonging to one organization from becoming accessible to another organization.

## 3. Role-Based Access Control

The company uses role-based access control to determine who can view or manage information.

The following roles are authorized to access this handbook:

- **Employee:** May read general workplace policies and procedures.
- **Manager:** May read employee policies and additional management guidance.
- **HR:** May read and manage HR-related policies and employee documentation.
- **Admin:** May manage document storage, permissions, metadata, and system-level configuration.

Access must be granted according to the principle of least privilege. Users should receive only the permissions required to perform their responsibilities.

Employees must not share their accounts or authentication credentials with other individuals. If an employee believes that they have been granted inappropriate access, they must notify HR or the system administrator.

Managers are responsible for ensuring that members of their teams understand the policies relevant to their work. However, managers may not grant system permissions unless their role explicitly includes that authority.

Administrative access is reserved for authorized personnel. Administrators may configure access controls, but they must still follow company security procedures and maintain an audit trail for significant changes.

## 4. Employee Conduct

All employees are expected to behave professionally, respectfully, and ethically.

Employees must:

1. Treat colleagues, customers, partners, and visitors with respect.
2. Follow applicable laws and company policies.
3. Protect company property and information.
4. Perform assigned responsibilities honestly and professionally.
5. Avoid behavior that creates a hostile, unsafe, discriminatory, or disruptive workplace.
6. Report suspected policy violations through appropriate channels.

Harassment, discrimination, intimidation, retaliation, threats, and deliberate misconduct are not permitted.

Employees should raise concerns promptly rather than ignoring serious policy violations. Reports should be made to a manager, HR representative, or another designated reporting channel.

The company prohibits retaliation against individuals who raise concerns in good faith or participate in an authorized investigation.

## 5. Working Hours and Attendance

Employees are expected to follow the working schedule established for their position and location.

Employees should begin work on time, remain available during agreed working hours, and communicate planned absences to their manager.

Unexpected absence should be communicated as soon as reasonably possible. Repeated unexplained absence or failure to follow attendance procedures may result in corrective action.

Some teams may operate flexible schedules. Flexible working arrangements do not remove the requirement to meet business commitments, attend required meetings, or remain reachable during agreed availability periods.

Managers should focus on work outcomes while ensuring that employees meet operational and collaboration requirements.

## 6. Leave Policy

Employees may request leave according to the organization's applicable leave categories and approval procedures.

Common categories may include:

- Annual or earned leave
- Sick leave
- Emergency leave
- Parental leave
- Bereavement leave
- Public holidays
- Other legally required or company-approved leave

Leave requests should normally be submitted through the designated HR or employee management system.

Planned leave should be requested in advance whenever possible. Managers should review requests based on team requirements, employee eligibility, and applicable company rules.

Employees must not assume that leave is approved until they receive confirmation through the designated process.

The organization will maintain appropriate records of leave requests and approvals. Sensitive personal information associated with leave must be handled only by authorized personnel.

## 7. Remote and Hybrid Work

Employees working remotely or under a hybrid arrangement are expected to maintain the same professional standards as employees working from company premises.

Employees must:

- Maintain a suitable and secure working environment.
- Protect company devices and documents from unauthorized access.
- Use approved company systems for business information.
- Follow required working hours and availability expectations.
- Protect confidential information when working in public spaces.
- Report lost devices or suspected security incidents immediately.

Company information should not be stored on personal devices or unauthorized cloud services unless specifically permitted by company policy.

Employees working remotely are responsible for maintaining appropriate internet connectivity and following company security requirements.

Managers may establish team-specific collaboration practices provided they do not conflict with company-wide policies.

## 8. Information Security and Confidentiality

Company information must be protected against unauthorized access, disclosure, modification, or loss.

Employees must treat confidential business information as restricted information. Confidential information may include customer information, employee records, financial information, business plans, source code, credentials, internal reports, contracts, and proprietary processes.

Employees must not:

- Share confidential documents with unauthorized individuals.
- Upload confidential information to unapproved services.
- Send sensitive information to personal email accounts.
- Leave confidential documents unattended in public areas.
- Attempt to bypass access controls.
- Use another person's credentials.

Passwords and authentication credentials must remain confidential. Where multi-factor authentication is available or required, employees must use it.

Any suspected security incident should be reported immediately to the designated security or administrative team.

## 10. Document Chunks and Access Metadata

Long documents may be divided into smaller passages, known as **chunks**, for search, retrieval, indexing, or AI-assisted processing.

Each chunk is a logical piece of text extracted from a source document. A chunk must retain enough metadata to establish where it originated and who may access it.

Every chunk must retain at least:

- `tenant`
- `document_id`
- `allowed_roles`
- `chunk_id`
- `text`
- Source document reference

For example, a chunk extracted from the Sample Leave Policy may contain:

**Chunk ID:** `CHUNK-DOC-TENA-002-001`  
**Tenant:** `tenant-a`  
**Document ID:** `DOC-TENA-002`  
**Allowed Roles:** `employee`, `manager`, `hr`  
**Text:** “Employees may request annual leave through the designated HR system...”

The permission information of a chunk must match the access restrictions of its source document.

A chunk belonging to `tenant-a` must never become searchable by a user who is authenticated only for `tenant-b`.

Similarly, if a document is restricted to the `hr` role, its extracted chunks must not be made available to ordinary employees through search, retrieval, embeddings, or AI-generated responses.

Chunk-level access control is particularly important when documents are stored in vector databases or retrieval systems. Metadata filtering must occur before restricted content is returned to a user.

## 11. AI and Search Systems

Where company documents are processed by search engines, knowledge bases, retrieval-augmented generation systems, or AI assistants, access permissions must be enforced at retrieval time.

A user asking a question does not automatically receive access to every document that contains a relevant answer.

The system must first determine:

1. The user's tenant.
2. The user's assigned roles.
3. The documents available to that tenant and those roles.
4. The chunks that satisfy the same access requirements.
5. Only then, the content that may be returned.

For example, an employee from `tenant-a` with the role `employee` may retrieve a chunk from a leave policy marked for `employee`, but should not retrieve a chunk from an HR investigation document restricted to `hr`.

The same principle applies to generated answers. An AI system must not reveal restricted information indirectly by summarizing, quoting, or combining unauthorized chunks.

## 12. Data Retention and Deletion

Company documents must be retained according to business, legal, contractual, and regulatory requirements.

When a document is deleted or access is revoked, associated chunks and indexed representations must also be removed or made inaccessible according to the organization's retention and deletion procedures.

Deleting the original document while leaving unrestricted copies of its chunks in a search index is not considered a complete deletion.

Administrators must periodically review stored documents and remove obsolete information where permitted by retention requirements.

## 13. Policy Violations

Failure to comply with company policies may result in corrective action.

Depending on the nature and severity of the violation, actions may include:

- Verbal or written warning
- Mandatory training
- Removal of system access
- Formal disciplinary action
- Termination of employment
- Legal action where appropriate

The company will consider relevant circumstances and applicable laws when responding to policy violations.

Security-related violations may result in immediate suspension of system access while an investigation is conducted.

## 14. Employee Responsibilities

Every employee is responsible for understanding and following the policies relevant to their role.

Employees should:

- Read applicable company policies.
- Protect company information.
- Use systems only for authorized purposes.
- Report security or policy concerns.
- Keep credentials secure.
- Respect access restrictions.
- Complete required compliance training.
- Ask HR or their manager when a policy is unclear.

Employees should never attempt to gain access to information simply because a technical system appears to permit it. Authorization is determined by business role and company policy, not merely by technical availability.

## 15. Manager Responsibilities

Managers are responsible for communicating relevant policies to their teams and ensuring that employees understand their responsibilities.

Managers should:

- Approve or reject requests according to applicable policies.
- Escalate serious employee or security concerns.
- Ensure team members have appropriate access.
- Avoid granting access beyond business requirements.
- Protect confidential employee information.
- Support compliance training and policy updates.

Managers must not share confidential HR information with employees who do not have a legitimate business need to access it.

---

## 16. Administration and Auditing

System administrators are responsible for maintaining the technical controls that support company policies.

Administrative activities should be auditable. Where appropriate, systems should record:

- Who accessed a document.
- Which document was accessed.
- When access occurred.
- Which role was used.
- Who changed permissions.
- Who created, modified, or deleted documents.
- Which chunks were indexed or removed.

Audit records must themselves be protected against unauthorized modification.

Periodic access reviews should be performed to identify users who have changed roles, left the organization, or no longer require specific permissions.
